# Goal planning and emergency funds

## Goal mapping
Goal-based planning links each investment to a specific goal (retirement, children's education, a home, a car) with a target amount and date. Mapping goals makes it easier to choose the right product and risk level for each goal and to track progress. A client with no goals mapped has investments without a clear purpose, which makes it hard to judge whether they are on track.

## Emergency fund
An emergency fund covers unexpected expenses or loss of income without forcing the sale of long-term investments. A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses (more for self-employed clients or single-income households), kept in liquid, low-risk instruments.

## Order of priorities
A widely used sequence is: emergency fund, then adequate protection (term and health insurance), then goal-based investing, and finally discretionary or higher-risk investments. Skipping protection exposes the whole plan to a single event such as illness or the death of an earning member.

## Reviewing goals
Goals, target amounts and timelines should be reviewed at least once a year and after life events, because inflation and changing priorities alter what is needed.
