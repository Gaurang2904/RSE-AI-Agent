# Life insurance savings plans

## What they are
Traditional life insurance savings plans (endowment, money-back and guaranteed savings plans) pay a benefit on maturity or on death during the policy term. They combine a modest amount of life cover with a savings element and often promise guaranteed or bonus-linked payouts.

## Strengths and limitations
They suit clients who value guaranteed, predictable payouts and forced long-term saving. Their returns are typically lower than long-term market-linked investments, the life cover they provide is small relative to the premium, and surrendering early can mean receiving much less than the premiums paid.

## How they fit in a plan
Savings plans should not replace adequate term cover for a client with dependants. A client paying a large annual savings-plan premium but holding no term plan may be under-insured; a review can compare the cover they actually have with what their dependants would need.

## Questions to check
What is the actual life cover? What is the internal rate of return if held to maturity? What are the surrender values in the early years? Does the client have enough liquidity elsewhere for emergencies?
