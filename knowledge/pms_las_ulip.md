# PMS, LAS and ULIPs

## Portfolio management services (PMS)
PMS is a professionally managed portfolio of securities held in the investor's own name, customised more than a mutual fund. In India PMS is regulated by SEBI, and the regulatory minimum investment has been ₹50 lakh (verify the current SEBI threshold before advising). PMS is usually suitable only for experienced investors with a high risk appetite, a long horizon and a large investable surplus. Fees can include fixed management fees and performance fees.

## Loan against securities (LAS)
LAS lets an investor borrow against shares, mutual funds or bonds without selling them. The lender applies a loan-to-value limit, and if the value of the pledged securities falls, the borrower may face a margin call and must add collateral or repay part of the loan, otherwise securities can be sold. LAS can meet short-term liquidity needs, but borrowing to invest increases risk.

## Unit-linked insurance plans (ULIPs)
A ULIP combines life insurance with market-linked investment: part of the premium buys life cover and the rest is invested in funds the policyholder chooses. ULIPs have a lock-in period of five years. Charges (premium allocation, policy administration, mortality and fund management charges) reduce returns, particularly in the early years. Life cover in a ULIP is usually much smaller than a term plan for the same premium.

## Choosing between them
These products carry different risks and costs. Suitability depends on the client's risk profile, liquidity needs, horizon and existing protection. Protection (term and health cover) is generally addressed before higher-risk investment products.
