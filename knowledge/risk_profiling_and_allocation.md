# Risk profiling and asset allocation

## Risk profile
A risk profile describes how much investment volatility a client can and is willing to accept. It is usually graded on a scale such as Conservative, Moderately Conservative, Moderate, Moderately Aggressive and Aggressive. It combines risk capacity (age, income stability, dependants, time horizon, existing wealth) with risk tolerance (the client's attitude to losses).

## Age and time horizon
As clients approach retirement, their capacity to recover from large losses generally falls because they have fewer earning years left and will soon need to draw on their savings. A shift toward more stable assets near retirement is a common principle. A client aged 56 or above with an aggressive or moderately aggressive profile is not automatically wrong, for example when they have large other assets or a long horizon for part of the money, but the profile should be reviewed and the reasons documented.

## Asset allocation by life stage
Younger investors with long horizons can usually hold a higher share of equity. Mid-career clients balance growth with protection and goal funding. Pre-retirement and retired clients typically raise the share of debt and stable income assets, keep an emergency fund, and plan withdrawals. These are general patterns, not rules for any individual.

## Reviewing profiles
Risk profiles become outdated as circumstances change. Re-assessing after major life events, and at regular intervals, keeps recommendations suitable.
