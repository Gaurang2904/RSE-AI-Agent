# SIP and SWP

## Systematic investment plan (SIP)
A SIP invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually monthly. It builds the habit of saving, removes the need to time the market, and spreads purchases across market levels. Because the same amount buys more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high, the average cost per unit is smoothed over time (rupee cost averaging). SIPs suit long-term goals such as retirement or children's education.

## Step-up SIP
A step-up (top-up) SIP increases the instalment periodically, for example by 10% each year, so contributions grow with income. Over long horizons, step-ups can make a large difference to the final corpus.

## Systematic withdrawal plan (SWP)
An SWP is the reverse of a SIP: the investor withdraws a fixed amount from a mutual fund at regular intervals while the rest stays invested. It is commonly used to create a regular income in retirement from an accumulated corpus. The withdrawal rate matters: withdrawing too much, especially after market falls early in retirement (sequence-of-returns risk), can deplete the corpus faster than planned.

## Difference between SIP and SWP
A SIP moves money from income into investments to accumulate wealth; an SWP moves money from investments into income to decumulate wealth. Clients often use SIPs during their earning years and switch to SWPs after retirement.

## Practical notes
Past returns do not guarantee future returns. Fund choice should match the client's risk profile and time horizon, and tax treatment of withdrawals depends on the fund type and holding period under current rules.
