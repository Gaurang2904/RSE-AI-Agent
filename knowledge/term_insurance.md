# Term insurance

## What it is
Term insurance is pure life cover for a fixed period (the term). If the insured person dies during the term, the insurer pays the sum assured to the nominees. If the person survives the term, nothing is paid back in a regular term plan. Because there is no savings component, term insurance usually gives the highest cover for the lowest premium of all life insurance types.

## Why clients with dependants need it
A person whose income supports others (a spouse, children, ageing parents) creates a financial risk for them: if that income stops, the family may not be able to meet living expenses, school fees or loan repayments. Term insurance replaces that income for the dependants. Clients with outstanding loans (home or business loans) also use term cover so that the debt does not pass to the family. Clients with no dependants and no debts generally have less need for large life cover.

## How much cover
A commonly used rule of thumb is a sum assured of roughly 10 to 15 times annual income, adjusted upward for outstanding loans and future goals (such as children's education) and downward for existing assets and other cover. Cover should be reviewed after major life events: marriage, a child's birth, a new home loan, or a large rise in income.

## Common features
Policies may offer riders such as accidental death benefit, critical illness cover or waiver of premium on disability. Premiums are generally lower when cover is bought at a younger age and in good health. Return-of-premium variants refund premiums at maturity but cost noticeably more than a regular term plan.

## Term cover versus savings plans
Combining insurance and investment in one product (endowment or savings plans, ULIPs) usually provides much less life cover per rupee of premium. A frequently recommended approach is to buy adequate term cover for protection and invest separately for wealth creation, though the right choice depends on each client's situation.
